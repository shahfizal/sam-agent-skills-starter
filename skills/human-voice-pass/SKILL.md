# Human Voice Pass

Marketing and social writing that sounds like a person, not a model.

## What this is

A writing and editing skill for marketing copy and any text that must sound human: social posts, captions, hooks, carousels, short-video scripts, emails, landing pages, cover letters, articles and Bahasa Melayu copy.

It has two jobs:

1. **Build the copy** — audience, one message, proof, call to action, platform shape.
2. **Give it a human voice** — remove generic AI phrasing, match the writer's voice from real samples, and add details only the writer can supply.

The core idea: do not "humanise" by sprinkling in quirks, slang or fake anecdotes. Remove what is generic, then add what only the writer knows. Voice comes from specific choices and specific knowledge, not decoration.

## Two modes

### Quick Voice Pass

Use when a draft already exists and needs to sound human. Run the seven-step pass below. Do not change the offer, the facts or the strategy.

### Campaign Build

Use when the user needs copy from scratch. Do the strategy step first, then write, then run the voice pass before delivery.

```
BRIEF
- Audience and moment:
- One message:
- Proof (only what is true):
- Offer and price:
- CTA (one action):
- Platform and format:
- Language:
- Must keep / must avoid:
```

If the brief is missing the audience, the offer or the CTA, ask before writing. A clever caption for an unclear offer is waste.

## Strategy before sentences

Every piece needs one job. Decide these before drafting:

- **Audience:** who, and what they currently believe.
- **One message:** if there are three, choose one.
- **Proof:** a fact, number, demo, customer words or a real before/after. No proof means no claim.
- **CTA:** one action, phrased in the words the customer would use.
- **Platform shape:** the native format, not a copy-paste of another platform.
- **Success signal:** saves, replies, clicks, bookings, leads.

## Platform shapes

| Platform | Shape | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram feed | Hook in line 1, one idea, 60–120 words, CTA, 3–5 relevant hashtags, alt text | The first line is the headline; do not bury it |
| Instagram carousel | Slide 1 = tension or promise; one idea per slide; last slide = CTA | No walls of text; 8 slides is usually too many |
| TikTok / Reels | Hook in 0–2 s, 3–5 beats, one payoff, on-screen text short | Write for the ear, not the page |
| Facebook | Story or useful detail first, then CTA; 80–150 words | Less hashtag, more conversation |
| LinkedIn | First 2 lines carry the post; short paragraphs; one lesson | No corporate throat-clearing |
| Threads | Conversational, one thought, invite a reply | Do not paste an Instagram caption |
| WhatsApp broadcast | Under 60 words, one link, permission-based | Never spam a list |
| Email | Subject + preview + one CTA; scannable | The preview line is part of the subject |
| Landing page | Headline, subhead, proof, objections, CTA | One page, one conversion |

## Hook bank

Use a pattern, then make it specific. Honest hooks beat fake urgency.

- **The mistake:** "Most [audience] get [thing] wrong in the same way."
- **The number:** "We cut [metric] from X to Y in Z weeks."
- **The before/after:** "What it looked like before, and what changed."
- **The objection:** "You think [objection]. Here is what actually happens."
- **The small truth:** "Nobody talks about the boring part of [thing]."
- **The customer question:** "A customer asked me this yesterday."
- **The contrarian:** "I do not recommend [common advice] for [specific case]."
- **The local detail:** a place, a price, a queue, a weather, a real scene.

Avoid: "In today's fast-paced world", "Unlock your potential", "Take it to the next level", "Game-changer", fake countdowns, invented scarcity.

## The voice pass

Run the steps in order. Do not skip to rewriting.

### 0. Freeze the facts

List what must survive unchanged: prices, names, dates, offers, links, claims, legal phrases. If a fact is missing, ask. Mark anything you cannot verify as `UNVERIFIED`.

### 1. Find the tells

Do not rewrite yet. Quote each phrase that sounds AI-written and give the reason.

Common tells:

- Filler openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape", "It is important to note that".
- Tidy summaries that repeat what was just said.
- Three-item lists everywhere, even when two or four items fit better.
- Every sentence the same length and rhythm.
- "Not just X, but Y" and "It's not about X, it's about Y" used as a reflex.
- Over-explaining the obvious.
- Empty transitions: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "That said".
- Hedging stacks: "may potentially help to facilitate".
- Generic calls to action: "Let's dive in", "Unlock your potential", "Take it to the next level".
- Perfectly balanced paragraphs with no point of view.
- No specifics: no names, places, numbers, mistakes or consequences.

> Read this text and mark every phrase that sounds AI-written: filler openers, tidy summaries, words nobody says out loud, three-item lists everywhere. Don't rewrite yet, just list them with the reason. Text: [paste]

### 2. Build the voice card

From three samples the writer actually wrote, extract:

```
VOICE CARD
- Typical sentence length:
- Short/long pattern:
- How they open:
- How they close:
- Contractions / particles:
- Words they actually use:
- Words they never use:
- Punctuation and formatting:
- Humour / formality:
- Language mix (EN / BM / code-switching):
```

Ask the writer to confirm or correct the card before rewriting. A wrong voice card produces a confident impression of the wrong person.

If there are no samples, say so and use a neutral human voice. Do not pretend to know their voice.

### 3. Rewrite in voice

Keep the facts locked. Prefer the writer's plainest word. If a phrase does not sound like them, cut it.

> Here are 3 things I wrote myself: [paste]. Study my sentence length, my words, how I open and close. Now rewrite this text in that voice. If a phrase doesn't sound like me, cut it. Text: [paste]

### 4. Break the rhythm

Vary the sentence length: some very short, some longer. Remove any sentence that only repeats the one before it.

> Rewrite this so sentence length varies: some very short, some longer. Remove any sentence that only repeats the one before it. Keep the meaning. Text: [paste]

Do not overcorrect into choppy fragments. Natural writing clusters: a long sentence, a short one, then a medium one.

### 5. Cut the wrapper

Delete the first and last sentence of each paragraph if the paragraph still works without them. Show what was cut.

> Delete the first and last sentence of every paragraph if the paragraph still works without them. Show me what you cut.

Most AI drafts start with a wind-up and end with a summary. Cutting both often removes a third of the text without losing meaning.

### 6. Add what only the writer knows

Ask five questions only someone with real experience could answer. Then weave the answers in as specific details, names and numbers. Do not invent the answers.

> Ask me 5 questions about this topic that only someone with real experience could answer. Then work my answers into the text as specific details, names and numbers. Don't invent any.

Good questions:

- What did a customer actually say, in their words?
- What went wrong the first time?
- What number surprised you?
- What do most people in this industry get wrong?
- What would you tell a friend over coffee that you would not put in a brochure?

### 7. Read it aloud

Read the draft as a skeptical colleague who spots AI writing instantly. Point to every sentence no one would say in a real conversation. Rewrite only those.

> Read this as a skeptical colleague who spots AI writing instantly. Point to every sentence you'd never hear in a real conversation and rewrite only those.

Then the writer reads it out loud once. If they stumble, the sentence is not finished.

## Bahasa Melayu notes

Match the writer's samples first. If the samples are limited, these are common AI-flavoured patterns and natural alternatives:

| AI-flavoured | More natural |
|---|---|
| Dalam era digital yang serba pantas ini | Sekarang |
| Adalah penting untuk ambil perhatian bahawa | Perlu diingat |
| Selain itu, ... (every paragraph) | Lagi satu..., Seterusnya..., or start directly |
| Justeru itu | Sebab itu, Jadi |
| Menerusi | Melalui, Dengan |
| Para pelanggan | Pelanggan |
| Melaksanakan | Buat, Jalankan |
| Mempunyai | Ada |
| Berupaya untuk | Boleh |
| Pada masa yang sama | Pada masa sama, Sementara itu |
| Yang mana | Yang |
| Sebagai seorang usahawan | (drop it if the context is clear) |
| Saya percaya bahawa | Saya rasa (only if the voice is conversational) |

Other Malaysian tells:

- Too many "yang" in one sentence. Split it.
- Literal translations: "leverage" becomes "gunakan", not "memanfaatkan sepenuhnya" every time.
- Over-formal address when the brand speaks casually.
- English-Malay code-switching that does not match the actual audience.
- Repeated "komprehensif", "holistik", "sinergi" with no concrete meaning.

Do not sand off regional words the writer actually uses. "Cikai", "boleh tahan", "memang", "je" may be right for one brand and wrong for another. The samples decide.

## Guardrails

- Preserve meaning. A voice edit that changes the offer, the claim or the price is a rewrite, not an edit; flag it.
- Never invent experience, testimonials, statistics, clients, results or urgency.
- Keep legal, medical and financial wording intact unless the writer explicitly asks to change it.
- Keep requested keywords and SEO phrases if the writer needs them, but make them fit the sentence.
- Do not add emojis, hashtags or slang unless they are already part of the writer's voice.
- If the draft is already good, say so and make fewer changes. Over-editing is its own tell.
- If the writer asks to imitate a named living writer, refuse the exact imitation and offer the underlying qualities instead.
- Disclose paid partnerships and gifted products where required.

## Quality gates

Before delivery, every marketing piece must pass:

- [ ] One audience, one message, one CTA
- [ ] The first line works as a hook on its own
- [ ] Every claim has proof or is removed
- [ ] No invented numbers, testimonials or urgency
- [ ] Platform-native length, ratio and formatting
- [ ] The copy sounds like the writer, not a model
- [ ] At least one specific detail the model could not invent
- [ ] Language reads naturally, not translated
- [ ] Alt text and accessibility considered
- [ ] Approval status clear before publishing
